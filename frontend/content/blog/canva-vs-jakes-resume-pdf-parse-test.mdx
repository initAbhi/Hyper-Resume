Every week we inspect dozens of fresher and early-career developer resumes across India. Good GitHub repos. Solid DSA prep. Yet their callback rate is near zero—not because of their skills, but because of what happens when an enterprise portal runs raw text extraction on their PDF.

## 1. What `pdf-parse` Actually Sees in a 2-Column Canva PDF

Canva is built for visual design, not structured document trees. When you export a 2-column resume with sidebar skill bars, icons, and floating text boxes, the underlying PDF text stream is often ordered by **visual Y-coordinate** rather than logical section flow.

Here is what a typical parser extracts from a 2-column layout:

```text
RAHUL SHARMA                    EXPERIENCE
B.Tech CSE (2025)               Backend Intern | Acme Corp
SKILLS                          Built REST APIs in Node.js
React, Java, Docker             EDUCATION
CONTACT                         CGPA: 8.4/10
```

Notice how `B.Tech CSE` gets glued next to `Backend Intern`, and `SKILLS` collides with `Built REST APIs`. When Workday's regex field-mapper tries to isolate your job title and employment dates, it fails silently.

<Callout type="warning" title="The 12 MB Canva Bloat + Image Compressor Trap">
Canva resumes with embedded portfolio thumbnails or custom fonts frequently export at **6–15 MB**. Many Indian corporate portals (Naukri, Superset, Workday) reject files over **2–5 MB**. Worse, if you run that PDF through a generic online compressor, it often rasterizes your resume into an image—leaving **0 selectable characters** for the ATS.
</Callout>

## 2. Single-Column `Jake's Resume`: Predictable Top-to-Bottom Stream

When we run a single-column `Jake's Resume` export (generated via headless Puppeteer with `tagged: true` and verified via `pdf-parse` for `extractableChars > 20`), the extracted text stream matches the exact order a human recruiter reads:

1. Header (Name, Email, GitHub, LinkedIn, Phone)
2. Education
3. Experience
4. Projects
5. Technical Skills

Zero column collisions. Zero icon-font garbage characters (`\uE801`).
